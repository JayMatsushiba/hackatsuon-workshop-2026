Why "Kazamachi"

風待ち kazamachi means "waiting for the wind." It is the old name of the inner-bay district of Kesennuma, from the centuries when sailing ships lay up in the harbour until the weather would let them leave. It is a good name for a company in a town where the answer to "what's for dinner" is still decided by the sea, and it is a reminder of how we work: we plan hard, and then we adapt to the day you actually get.

What we do

Two services, one aim

Kesennuma is a rewarding place and a genuinely difficult one to plan from abroad. The information is mostly in Japanese, the good experiences have booking lead times and two-person minimums, and the timetables changed again this year.

Service 01

Custom itinerary design

You tell us your dates, your pace, who is coming and what you care about. We come back with a day-by-day plan built around what is actually landing, open and running that week — with the bookings made, the closures avoided, and the transport that exists rather than the transport the brochures describe.

Delivered as a document you can read on your phone, in English, with maps, walking times, and a plan B for rain.

Service 02

Guiding, in English

A guide is the difference between watching the auction floor and understanding what the buyers are doing with their hands. We guide half-days and full days: the market before dawn, the inner-bay district, the coastal trail on Karakuwa, Ōshima island, the memorial sites.

We interpret, we introduce you to people, and we handle the phone calls that would otherwise be impossible.

Things we are asked for often

  • A single perfect day, for people passing through on the Sanriku coast
  • Car-free weekends built around e-bikes, the BRT and one taxi
  • Seasonal food trips: bonito, saury, winter swordfish, oysters
  • Disaster-learning visits with a kataribe storyteller, arranged and interpreted with care
  • Hiking support on the Michinoku Coastal Trail and the Miyagi Olle course, including luggage logistics
  • Photography and writing trips that need access rather than viewpoints

Things we will tell you honestly

  • If your dates fall outside a season, we will say so rather than sell you the wrong month
  • If a guide adds nothing to what you want to do, we will say that too
  • If an experience only makes sense as a group charter, you will hear the real number before you commit
  • If the weather is going to close the monorail or the boat, we will have already planned the alternative

How it works

Four steps, no obligation until step three

1. Tell us
Send your dates, group size, interests, mobility needs, dietary needs, and whether you will have a car. A paragraph is enough to start.
2. We sketch
Within a few working days you get an outline plan and a clear price, free of charge. If it is not what you wanted, tell us and we will redraw it.
3. You confirm
You approve the plan and pay. We then make the bookings that need lead time — some are three days out, some two weeks.
4. You travel
You get the final itinerary before you fly, and a phone number that answers in English while you are here.

Practicalities

The details people ask about

Languages
English and Japanese. Placeholder: list any other languages actually offered, and whether they are in-house or via a partner interpreter.
Group size
Built for one to eight people. Larger groups are possible and are quoted differently, because several local experiences are priced per boat or per group rather than per person.
Families
Welcome, with two real constraints: sea kayaking here is junior-high age and up, and the oyster-raft boat trip has a minimum age. Kameyama Terrace's monorail is step-free, and Kodanohama is a shallow, lifeguarded, jet-ski-free beach in season.
Accessibility
The new Kameyama monorail, the bay cruise, the memorial museum and the inner-bay district all work reasonably well for visitors with limited mobility. Clifftop trails and the market floor do not. Tell us what you need and we will be specific rather than optimistic.
Dietary needs
This is a seafood town, which makes some requests easy and others hard. There is a halal-certified restaurant and a prayer room by the fish market, and one hotel in town markets itself as Muslim-friendly. Vegetarian and vegan visitors should talk to us early — it is workable with notice and awkward without.
Cash
Bring some. Card acceptance at small independent restaurants and market stalls is inconsistent, and the city's local buses are cash only even though the BRT takes Suica.
What we are not
We are not a booking site, a hotel, or a coach operator. We do not run our own boats or vehicles. We work with the operators, the visitor centres and the tourism bureau who do.
