# Research notes — Kesennuma tourism site

Raw research behind the copy in `index.html`, `itineraries.html` and `about.html`.
Gathered on **2026-07-25** by four parallel research agents, each working a different
angle from official Japanese and English sources.

These are working notes, not publishable copy. They are deliberately verbose and
deliberately full of caveats — the point of keeping them is that the site's copy is
conservative, and if you later want to add a price, a timetable or a superlative, this
is where you find out how solid the ground under it actually is.

## How to read the confidence tags

Each agent used a slightly different notation, but they all mean the same three things:

- **`[C]` / `[CONFIRMED]` / `[F]`** — the agent fetched the page and read it directly
- **`[S]` / `[SEARCH-ONLY]`** — seen only in a search-result snippet; a lead, not a fact
- **`[U]` / `[UNVERIFIED]`** — could not confirm, or sources contradicted each other
- **`[VERIFY]` / `⚠VERIFY`** — volatile (prices, hours, timetables, event dates); re-check before publishing
- **`[C-tr]`** — confirmed, but through machine translation of a Japanese page, so wording may be loose

## Things the research established that contradict published English sources

Worth re-reading before editing the site, because most English material online is wrong
on at least one of these:

1. **No scheduled ferry to Ōshima.** The ~100-year-old service ended 2019-04-07, the day
   the Ōshima Ōhashi bridge opened. Only sightseeing cruises remain, on a partial
   weekend schedule.
2. **Mt Kameyama has a monorail, not a chairlift or a shuttle bus.** Kameyama Terrace 360°
   opened 2026-07-19; the summit paths had been closed for construction since
   September 2024.
3. **The Karakuwa visitor centre's tsunami-simulation hall is permanently closed** (final
   performance June 2022), though the centre itself reopened after renovation in
   April 2024.
4. **The Shark Museum is now about sharks.** Its 2011 disaster-and-recovery zones were
   replaced in the 2024 renewal.
5. **Kesennuma's 28-year run as Japan's top fresh-bonito port broke with the poor 2025
   season.** Official tourism pages and regional news disagree about whether it has been
   reclaimed. Describe the run, not the current rank.
6. **The Reconstruction Yatai Village ("Kesennuma Yokocho") no longer exists** — it closed
   in March 2017.
7. **`kesennuma-love.com` is dead** (DNS does not resolve) despite ranking well in search.

## Unresolved questions

The biggest one, flagged by two agents independently: **weekend public transport to Ōshima
and around Karakuwa.** The official English page says the Ōshima citizen bus runs weekdays
only, the 2026 beach page implies weekend services in swimming season, and the Karakuwa
routes appear to have been partly restructured into demand-responsive transport. The site
copy works around this by recommending e-bikes, a car or a taxi and telling readers to
check a current timetable. If you ever want to publish a bus-based itinerary, resolve this
with the tourism bureau first.
