# Kesennuma — Access, Local Transport, Practical Basics, and a Survey of Existing Tourism Websites

**Research date:** 2026-07-25. Everything below distinguishes pages actually fetched from search-snippet leads. All fares, timetables and hours must be re-verified before publishing.

---

## A. Existing tourism web presence

**1. kesennuma-kanko.jp — 「気仙沼さ来てけらいん」(official, Japanese)**
Run by 気仙沼観光推進機構 (Kesennuma Tourism & Convention Bureau / Tourism Promotion Organization), 〒988-0037 気仙沼市魚市場前7-13 (i.e. at Umi no Ichi). Nav: seasonal picks, 観光スポット, グルメ, 体験・ツアー, 泊まる, イベント, モデルコース, アクセス. Footer copyright says 2014 but content is genuinely current (news 2026-06-26 on regaining #1 fresh-bonito landings; 亀山テラス360° viewpoint opening July 19, 2026).
*Covers well:* the best accommodation database for the area (typed categories + amenity filters incl. Wi-Fi, 天然温泉, 大浴場, 禁煙, ペットOK, by district 気仙沼/階上/大島/唐桑); the access page carries a rental-car operator list (10+), taxi companies by district, city bus timetables, rental cycle at five locations ¥500–¥1,500, and parking rules (station-front lot 30 min free then ¥100/hr; inner-bay lot 3 hrs free off-peak).
*Covers badly:* zero English. Its access page does **not** actually spell out routes from Tokyo/Sendai/Ichinoseki — it just links out to bus/train sites.

**2. visit-kesennuma.com — "VISIT KESENNUMA: A Fishing Port in Harmony with the Ocean" (official English)**
Operated by the Kesennuma Tourism Authority, © 2020. Languages: EN, TH, zh-TW, KO, ID. Nav: About (Why Kesennuma?, Get Inspired, Annual Events) / Top Picks / Plan Your Trip (Trekking, Learn About Our Tsunami Story, Cycling Adventures, an "A.I. Route Planner", "Add to My Plan" favouriting) / Travel Info / See & Do (Things to Do, Places to See, Food & Drink, Seasonal Events, Accommodation).
*Covers well:* thematic inspiration; a month-by-month annual events calendar; individual attraction pages; multilingual reach.
*Covers badly — this is the gap our site fills:* "Travel Info" contains **only** an ACCESS page. No tourist-information-centre page, no Wi-Fi, no money/cards, no medical/emergency, no local-bus explanation. The ACCESS page is diagram-led and **demonstrably out of date**: it lists rental bikes at *two* locations for ¥500/¥700 per day, while the Japanese official site lists *five* locations at ¥500–¥1,500; it gives the station TIC phone as 0226-22-4699 where Kesennuma City's own page says 0226-22-4669. `/see-do/accommodation/` returns 404. English is competent but marketing-flavoured and vague on logistics.

**3. kesennuma-love.com — DEAD.** Still appears at the top of search results labelled "Kesennuma Travel Guide — Kesennuma Tourism & Convention Bureau", but DNS does not resolve (`ENOTFOUND`). Do not link it.

**4. Kesennuma City official — kesennuma.miyagi.jp**
Japanese municipal site. A "Foreign Language" link (`/foreign.html`) offers **machine translation only** (EN/ZH/KO) with an explicit disclaimer about inaccuracy. `外国人住民向け` is for *residents* (residence cards, address registration), not tourists. Tourism is deliberately delegated to kesennuma-kanko.jp. **However**, the single most current and authoritative transport page anywhere is the city's `市内公共交通時刻表` page (page updated **2026-06-26**): JR Ofunato Line (timetable dated 2026-03-14), Kesennuma Line BRT (→Maeyachi), Ofunato Line BRT (→Sakari, incl. 鹿折金山線), 中央循環線 circulating bus (operator Kesennuma Haitaku, fares updated 2026-03-01), Miyako Bus lines (御崎/大沢/新月/松岩/三陸/大島/本吉内陸/本吉川内), and shared taxis 乗合タクシー (上八瀬, 金沢, 羽田, 九条 — converted to shared taxi 2026-04-01, 本吉三陸). Facility page for 気仙沼駅前観光案内所 last updated 2016-09-13.

**5. kesennuma-memorial.jp/english/ — Ruins of the Great East Japan Earthquake Kesennuma City Memorial Museum**
Run by Kesennuma City. **The best-executed English access copy in the area** and a strong structural model: organised by origin (From Sendai / From Ichinoseki), by mode, with parking counts and a taxi-discount note.

**6. visitmiyagi.com — VISIT MIYAGI**
Miyagi Prefectural Government, © 2018. EN/FR/TH/KO/zh-TW/zh-CN. Six area hubs (Sendai, Matsushima Bay, **Sanriku Coast**, Mt Zao, Northern Miyagi, Near Miyagi) + categories + Trip Planner. Kesennuma items: Fish Market, Scenic Oshima Island, Kesennuma-Karakuwa Olle Trail, Kodanohama Beach, halal/Muslim-friendly Kesennuma Plaza Hotel, "Sea-to-Table in Kesennuma" and "Sanriku Coastal Charm" itineraries; also hosts a Miyagi Olle Trail Guidebook PDF (1st edition Sept 2019).
*Best-written English of the government sites* — clean, edited, short. *Weak:* article-level, no comprehensive Kesennuma guide; access is a one-line footnote per article; the prefecture-level Access page only covers Sendai/shinkansen/airport. Minor sloppiness ("Minami-Kesenuma").

**7. tohokukanko.jp/en — TRAVEL TO TOHOKU**
Tohoku Tourism Promotion Organization (東北観光推進機構). Six languages. Nav: Destinations, Features, Itineraries, What to See & Do, Dining, Transport & Accommodation, Getting to Tohoku — including a genuinely useful operator directory (Sendai Airport Transit, Miyagi Transportation, 大島汽船). ~10 Kesennuma entries: Oshima, Earthquake Disaster Memorial Park, Umi no Ichi ("Sea Market"), Kesennuma Bay Cruise, Rias Ark Museum, Naiwan (inner bay), Memorial Museum, Bay Crossing Bridge, Brewer's Table.
*English:* professional but occasionally awkward and sometimes truncated mid-sentence. *Staleness proof point:* its 唐桑半島ビジターセンター entry is still flagged 【現在休館中】 ("currently closed") although the official Japanese page says it reopened after renovation on 2024-04-20.

**8. JNTO — japan.travel/en/spot/2128/**
Thin: a few paragraphs (Mt Anba 239 m, Cape Iwaisaki blowhole, Oshima via bridge, Rias Ark, K-Port café) plus a two-line "How to Get There". References "new programs developed since 2017" — looks unrefreshed. Its bus line ("from the west exit of Sendai Station to Kesennuma City Hall, about three hours") is loose; the service actually boards at the Miyakoh Sendai Express Bus Center.

**9. japan-guide.com — the most useful English practical source currently online**
Independent commercial site; native-quality English. `/e/e5036.html` Kesennuma (last updated **2025-02-20**), `/e/e5037.html` Rias Ark (2025-02-20), `/e/e5026.html` "Sanriku Coast: Access, Orientation and Transportation" (2025-02-12).
**Tone model to emulate:** plain declarative sentences; always states duration + fare + frequency + pass validity together; explicitly flags constraints ("one train every 1-2 hours"); no marketing adjectives. *Weak:* no depth on Karakuwa, accommodation, or food.

**10. en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kesennuma** — volunteer; has Get in / Get around / Sleep / Eat, and is the only source with the municipal circulating bus described in English. But contains outright errors (says the nearest expressway is "in Ichinoseki, about an hour's drive **east**" — Ichinoseki is west, and the Sanriku Expressway now runs through the city) and stale listings. Leads only.

**11. Hiking sites — the strongest niche content:**
- **m-tc.org/en** — 認定NPO法人みちのくトレイルクラブ (Natori City), official Michinoku Coastal Trail manager. Area 5: **102 km of MCT inside Kesennuma City, the longest continuous section in any single municipality**; Karakuwa Peninsula 19 km, Oshima 18 km, 88 km road-walking total, a 17 km stretch Shark Museum→Oya Beach; Mt Tatsugane 511 m, Mt Kameyama 235 m; BRT Matsuiwa and BRT Oya Kaigan as trailhead access; resupply warning (Motoyoshi supermarket before a 32 km gap). Professional, lightly translated English.
- **michinokutrail.com/kesennuma-oshima** — separate volunteer/donation-funded English site. Excellent honest tone: Oshima loop 18.2 km, 530 m ascent / 550 m descent, intermediate; bus from Kesennuma Station ~30 min but "there are only a handful every day"; ~10 km cycle from the station.
- **miyagiolle.jp/en/course/kesennuma/** — Miyagi Olle Trail, run by the **Management Tourism Division, Miyagi Prefectural Government**. Kesennuma/Karakuwa course 10.0 km, Visitor Center → Hanzo, with waypoint distances (Osaki Shrine 0.4, Cape Osaki 0.8, Kannokura Tsunami Stones 3.0, Tsubaki Tunnel 3.8, Oreishi 8.0, Hanzo 10.0) and **eight local taxi companies with phone numbers**. Comprehensive, professional English.

**12. "Sanriku" regional sites — structural gap worth exploiting**
- **sanriku-travel.jp/en** ("Sanriku Tabishirube", Sanriku Fund Association / Sanriku DMO Center, Miyako City) covers **Iwate's Sanriku coast only** — Hirono/Kuji/Noda/Fudai, Tanohata/Iwaizumi/Miyako/Yamada, Otsuchi/Kamaishi/Sumita/Ofunato/Rikuzentakata. **It does not include Kesennuma.**
- **sanrikufukkonationalpark.com/en** (Sanriku Fukko National Park Association, office in Miyako City Tourism Division) — park-wide (28,539 ha), good English, extensive access info; Kesennuma coverage limited to Mt Tokusenjo azaleas and Cape Osaki.
- Net effect: **the Sanriku DMO is Iwate-facing and Kesennuma falls between it and Miyagi Prefecture's coverage.** No regional site treats Kesennuma as a hub, even though it is the BRT interchange for the whole coast.

**13. Other English-language Kesennuma content**
- **pref.miyagi.jp/site/miyagifukkounotabi/en/course/kesennuma.html** — "Miyagi: The Road to Recovery", Miyagi Prefecture Reconstruction Support and Memorial Division. Two-day, entirely car-based Kesennuma–Minamisanriku model course with drive times. Good English; **stale** (latest reference "By the end of 2021").
- **kesennuma-t.co.jp/kesennuma_tour_english.html** — Kesennuma Tourism Touroperating LLC / 気仙沼ヘルスツーリズム協議会, registered travel agency No. 29 (Miyagi Governor), 2-2-13 Yokamachi, Kesennuma 988-0084. Health tourism (fishing, farming, forest/ocean therapy, yoga), 2-hour to full-day experiences, education travel, telework, vacant-house long-stay, corporate training, disaster-prevention storytelling. Written ~2024 ("thirteen years have passed…"). Professional but translated-feeling.
- **fun-japan.jp/en/articles/9907** (JTB Corp, published 2019-06-30) — unusually rich practical detail for an English article: Shark Museum "credit cards not accepted; free Wi-Fi available"; Fish Market 354 m 2F observation deck at 8-25 Uoichibamae; Kesennuma Plaza Hotel has English-speaking staff, hot springs, free station shuttle; Memorial Museum exhibits have foreign-language explanations. **2019 — verify everything.**
- **madeinmiyagi.wordpress.com/area-guides/kesennuma-area/** — JET-community blog, most recent posts ~2014. Broad and readable (even lists English-speaking medical providers) but badly stale; quotes Sendai express bus ¥1,800/~3 hrs and Tokyo night bus ¥15,000 return, both superseded.
- **crewship.net — KESENNUMA Crewship**: local loyalty card + app, 68,069 members, **Japanese only, no English** — yet visit-kesennuma.com promotes a "Kesennuma Crewcard" app to foreign visitors.
- **oshima-kanko.jp** (Kesennuma Tourism Association Oshima Branch): HTTP-only, SSL handshake fails, appears abandoned legacy.
- No original reporting: Tripadvisor, Expedia, KAYAK, Trip.com, travelinjapan.net. `japantravel.navitime.com` is useful as an English BRT/bus **timetable tool**, not a guide.

**"Kesennuma Crossing": I could not find any website or tourism brand by that name.** Every search for the phrase returns the **Kesennuma Bay Crossing Bridge** (気仙沼湾横断橋, nicknamed かなえおおはし *Kanae Ohashi*) — 1,344 m, longest cable-stayed bridge in Tohoku, opened March 2021 with the Kesennuma IC–Karakuwa Peninsula IC section, floodlit at night. Possible confusion with "KESENNUMA Crewship"/"Crewcard".

**What the good ones do (for our copy):** organise access **by origin city**, not by mode; give duration + fare + frequency + pass validity in one breath; name the exact station/stop and the walk from it; state constraints honestly ("only a handful of buses per day", "buses do not take IC cards"); include parking counts and phone numbers; date the information.

## B. Getting there

**Confirmed by sources I fetched (all figures: verify before publishing):**

- **Tokyo → Kesennuma, the standard route:** Tohoku Shinkansen to **Ichinoseki (一ノ関)**, transfer to the **JR Ofunato Line** to Kesennuma. japan-guide (updated 2025-02-20): Ichinoseki→Kesennuma **85 minutes, ¥1,230 one way, one train every 1–2 hours**; covered by Japan Rail Pass, JR East Pass, JR East South Hokkaido Pass. Kesennuma Tourism Touroperating: "approximately 4 hours from Tokyo by bullet train."
- **Ofunato Line rail section** (Wikipedia): Ichinoseki–Kesennuma survives as railway, **62.0 km, 14 stations** (Ichinoseki, Mataki, Rikuchū-Kanzaki, Iwanoshita, Rikuchū-Matsukawa, Geibikei, Shibajuku, Surisawa, Senmaya, Konashi, Yagoshi, Orikabe, Niitsuki, Kesennuma), KiHa 100 series DMUs; roughly ten eastbound local + one *Super Dragon* rapid, nine westbound local + one rapid. Kesennuma–Sakari was **not** rebuilt as rail.
- **Sendai → Kesennuma by rail:** JNTO: "From Sendai, take the bullet train to Ichinoseki, then transfer to the Ofunato line… Total travel time is about two hours." Kesennuma Tourism Touroperating: "under 2 hours from Sendai."
- **Ichinoseki → Kesennuma by bus:** japan-guide: **Iwateken Kotsu, 2–4 buses/day, ~75 minutes, ¥1,090**.
- **Highway bus Sendai ↔ Kesennuma:** operator **宮城交通 Miyagi Kotsu (Miyakoh)**, route 「仙台－気仙沼・南三陸（自由乗車制）」 via Sendai-Tobu Road + Sanriku Expressway. From miyakou.co.jp: **5 round trips/day**; stops incl. 県庁市役所, Sendai Station, Sansan Minamisanriku, Motoyoshi Toyosawa, Ohtani Coast, Minami-Kesennuma Station; **~2.5 hours** Sendai→Minami-Kesennuma; **fares as of 2024-10-01: Sendai→Sansan Minamisanriku ¥1,900, Sendai→Kesennuma area ¥2,200**, 2-ride return tickets ¥3,500–¥4,100, 50% child/disabled discount; **no advance reservation, first-come seating**; timetable effective 2025-04-01, Sendai departures 10:40/14:00/15:05/17:10/19:15, returns 05:55/06:55/08:25/12:25/14:25. Boards at the **Miyakoh Sendai Express Bus Center**, stop 40 (per kesennuma-memorial.jp). *Conflicts:* Wikivoyage says six/day; JNTO and Miyagi Olle both say "about three hours" — resolve with the operator.
- **Overnight/highway bus to Tokyo: NOT CONFIRMED.** Wikivoyage claims a nightly Kesennuma↔Ikebukuro service; japanbusonline's Kesennuma→Tokyo search returned only Sendai–Tokyo services plus a "Kesen Liner / Tono-Kamaishi Liner" (Iwate–Tokyo). I could not confirm a current direct Kesennuma–Tokyo bus. Do not publish without checking.
- **Nearest airport: Sendai (SDJ).** Sendai Airport Access Line, operated by Sendai Airport Transit Co. with JR East through service to Sendai Station: **local 24–29 min**; the 17-minute rapid is being discontinued — **as of March 2026 all services are unified as local**. Roughly every 20 min 09:00–17:00, every 30 min otherwise (Wikipedia). visit-kesennuma also lists **Iwate Hanamaki Airport** as an option; rental cars available at both airports.
- **Driving:** the **Sanriku Expressway (三陸道)** Miyagi section (~126 km) was completed in March 2021 with the Kesennuma IC–Karakuwa Peninsula IC segment including the Bay Crossing Bridge. japan-guide: **toll-free beyond Matsushima**. Drive times seen on sources: Sendai→Kesennuma Bay Crossing Bridge **1 hr 45 min** (Miyagi Reconstruction site); Sendai Airport→Karakuwa Peninsula IC **~2 hours**, then ~20 min (Miyagi Olle). Relevant ICs: Kesennuma IC, Kesennuma Port IC, Urashima-Oshima IC, Kesennuma-Kashiori IC, Oya-kaigan IC, Karakuwa Peninsula IC, Rikuzen-Takata Nagabe IC.
- **BRT — Kesennuma is the hinge of the whole coast:**
  - **Kesennuma Line BRT**: Kesennuma ↔ **Maeyachi (前谷地)**, 72.8 km, 26 stops, opened 2012-08-20. At Maeyachi connect to rail toward Ishinomaki/Sendai.
  - **Ofunato Line BRT**: Kesennuma ↔ **Sakari (盛)**, 43.7 km, 26 stops, opened 2013-03-02, via Rikuzentakata / Miracle Lone Pine / Ofunato.
  - Both JR East. **Level 2 self-driving since December 2022**; ~50+ km of dedicated former-railbed busway, with extensions completed in 2024.
  - **Japan Rail Pass, JR East Pass and Seishun 18 Kippu are valid on the BRT** (japan-guide e5026, 2025-02-12). Note the Sanriku Railway further north takes JR East Pass but **not** the Japan Rail Pass.
  - **Suica accepted since 2015-03-14**; local **odeca** IC card since 2013-08-03; cash accepted onboard; ordinary JR paper tickets valid. **You cannot combine the Sendai Suica area and the BRT area in one IC journey — buy a paper ticket for through trips.**
  - **Kesennuma Station**: 1-5-25 Furumachi, Kesennuma 988-0077; platform 1 = Kesennuma Line BRT, platform 2 = BRT siding, platform 3 = Ofunato Line rail toward Ichinoseki; staffed with a Midori no Madoguchi. Wikivoyage says the station is open 05:00–20:00 (unverified).
  - **Caveat:** JR East's own English BRT pages (`jreast.co.jp/en/railway/train/brt/` and `/guide.html`) returned **HTTP 403** to me. Nothing above is verified directly from the operator's English pages.

**Plausible but unverified:** exact Tokyo→Ichinoseki shinkansen times and fares (I did not fetch a source); whether the Ofunato Line has convenient same-platform connections from arriving shinkansen; current existence of a direct Tokyo–Kesennuma bus.

## C. Getting around locally

- **Is a car effectively necessary? Largely yes, outside the station/inner-bay/Umi no Ichi core.** japan-guide (e5026): rental cars give flexibility because "public transportation along the Sanriku Coast had been rather infrequent and slow." Even the Rias Ark Museum is "reached from Kesennuma Station by taxi in about 20 minutes and for around 2,400 yen one way." The Miyagi Prefecture recovery itinerary for Kesennuma is 100% car-based. Karakuwa, Oya Beach and Iwaisaki are impractical on a tight schedule without a car.
- **Rental cars in Kesennuma:** kesennuma-kanko.jp's access page lists **10+ operators**. Search-level (verify): Toyota Rent a Car at 4-1-5 Tanakamae, Kesennuma (no customer parking); Nippon Rent-A-Car at 4-4-27 Saiwai-cho, tel 0226-22-8190. Also rentable at Sendai Airport, Sendai, Ichinoseki, Morioka.
- **City circulating bus:** 中央循環線 ("Central Circulation Line"), a city-hospital loop, operated by **Kesennuma Haitaku**; fares updated 2026-03-01 (city page). Wikivoyage (verify): six runs/day each direction, stopping at the station, city hall, Umi no Ichi, downtown Tanaka-mae and the city hospital; **¥200 adult / ¥100 child**.
- **Miyako Bus (ミヤコーバス)**, Kesennuma office tel 0226-22-7163. Lines per the city page: 御崎 (Osaki), 大沢, 新月, 松岩, 三陸, **大島 (Oshima)**, 本吉内陸, 本吉川内.
- **Shared taxis (乗合タクシー)** run fixed routes at fixed times with no reservation, like buses: 上八瀬, 金沢, 羽田, 九条 (converted from bus 2026-04-01), 本吉三陸.
- **Payment gotcha worth its own line of copy:** **Kesennuma's local buses do not accept IC cards — cash only** (visit-kesennuma). The **BRT does** accept Suica. Same city, opposite rules.
- **Taxis:** kesennuma-kanko lists companies across three districts; Miyagi Olle lists eight with phone numbers. The Memorial Museum offers a **¥500 taxi discount each way (¥1,000 total)**.
- **Rental bicycles — sources actively conflict; must be verified with the bureau:**
  - Japanese official (kesennuma-kanko news post, and its access page): three/five locations — **Kesennuma Ekimae Tourist Information Center** (4 regular + 6 e-assist), **Kesennuma City Tourism Service Center, Umi no Ichi 2F** (7 e-assist), **Oshima Welcome Terminal** (5 e-assist); **hours 09:00–17:00**; **e-assist ¥1,500/day or ¥800/2 hrs, regular ¥1,000/day or ¥500/2 hrs, one-way drop-off +¥500**; returnable at any location. The access page summarises the range as ¥500–¥1,500 across five locations.
  - English visit-kesennuma access page: **¥500/day regular, ¥700/day e-assist, two locations, 8:30am–5:15pm, no reservation needed** — apparently pre-revision.
  - A Japanese notice also restricted rental days at the station office (Mon/Tue/Fri/Sat/Sun; staff absent Wed/Thu).
- **Oshima island (大島):**
  - **Reached by road bridge — the Kesennuma Oshima Ohashi (opened April 2019), nickname Tsurukame Ohashi 鶴亀大橋**, 297 m arch span, third-longest arch bridge in Japan; turnouts at both ends so you can park and walk across.
  - **The regular passenger ferry is gone** — Oshima Kisen's ~100-year-old scheduled route ended **2019-04-07**. Oshima Kisen now runs the tourist **Kesennuma Bay Cruise (気仙沼ベイクルーズ)**: ~50 min, passing under both the Oshima Ohashi and the Bay Crossing Bridge, **April–November, Saturdays/Sundays/holidays, 5 departures/day (6 in Golden Week and Obon)** — verify seasonally at oshimakisen.com. Do not tell readers there is a ferry to the island.
  - **By bus:** 大島線, Kesennuma Station ↔ **浦の浜 Uranohama (Oshima Welcome Terminal)**, **~30 minutes**, "only a handful every day"; route also serves 東新城一丁目, 市役所前, 鹿折唐桑駅前, 浪板, 要害, 大島出張所前. visitmiyagi also gives ~30 min by bus from **Shishiori-Karakuwa Station (鹿折唐桑駅)**.
  - **By bike:** ~10 km from Kesennuma Station.
  - **By car:** 7 min from Urashima-Oshima IC (from Sendai direction); 9 min from Kesennuma-Kashiori IC (from Iwate direction).
  - **Oshima Welcome Terminal**: 気仙沼市浦の浜151-2外, 09:00–17:00, tel 0226-28-9253, ~100 parking spaces; local produce/souvenir sales, terrace over Uranohama Bay, multipurpose space; adjacent 野杜海 (Nodoka) complex with six shops incl. restaurants and a fishmonger. Doubles as an information hub and bike-rental point.
  - On-island: Kodanohama (小田の浜) beach; Mt Kameyama (235 m) with the new **亀山テラス360°** viewpoint opening 2026-07-19; 18.2 km loop hike; HATOBA Café (coffee jelly), Gold Smile seafood BBQ, Nagame café/gallery; 休暇村気仙沼大島 (Kyukamura), Oshima Campsite, グランピング&キャンプ CAMELLIA気仙沼大島.
- **Karakuwa peninsula (唐桑):**
  - **By bus:** Miyako Bus **御崎線 (Osaki line)** bound for 御崎, boarding at **三日町 Mikkamachi** or **気仙沼市役所前 (City Hall)** — **note the gotcha: these stops are a ~10-minute walk from Kesennuma Station, not at it** — then **~45 minutes** to the 唐桑半島ビジターセンター stop.
  - **By car:** 18 min from Karakuwa Peninsula IC (Sanriku Expressway, from Sendai); 25 min from Rikuzen-Takata Nagabe IC.
  - **Karakuwa Peninsula Visitor Center**: 気仙沼市唐桑町崎浜4-3, **09:00–16:30**, closed Tuesdays (next day if a holiday), the day after public holidays (open if that falls on Sat/Sun), and Dec 29–Jan 3; **free admission**; tel 0226-32-3029; 20 parking spaces; **reopened after renovation 2024-04-20**. **The 津波体験館 (Tsunami Experience Hall) has permanently closed** — several sites still list it.
  - Sights: 巨釜 Ogama rock formations, **折石 Oreishi** (16 m × 3 m marble column, top broken in the 1896 tsunami), 大理石海岸 Dairiseki Coast, 御崎 Cape Osaki (250-million-year sedimentary rock), 唐桑御崎神社, Kannokura Tsunami Stones (150-ton boulders moved in 2011), Tsubaki Tunnel (20,000+ camellias, blooms March–April). Miyagi Olle 10 km course and 19 km of Michinoku Coastal Trail natural track.

## D. Practical basics for foreign visitors

**English signage and support — poorly documented; do not overstate.** The only things I could confirm: the Kesennuma City Memorial Museum has **foreign-language exhibit explanations**; the **Kesennuma Tourism & Convention Bureau offers service in English**, with an English-capable email address and online tour booking (JNTO); **Kesennuma Plaza Hotel has English-speaking staff** and a free station shuttle (fun-japan, 2019). Against that: Kesennuma City's website is machine-translation-only with a disclaimer, and the local Crewship card scheme is Japanese-only. **No source I fetched documents station signage, street signage, English menus, or English at bus stops.** This needs on-the-ground checking before we write anything reassuring.

**Tourist information centres (confirmed):**
1. **気仙沼駅前観光案内所 — Kesennuma Ekimae (Station Front) Tourist Information Center.** 気仙沼市古町1丁目5-23, in front of JR Kesennuma Station. **Phone conflict: 0226-22-4669** (Kesennuma City page, itself last updated 2016-09-13) **vs 0226-22-4699** (visit-kesennuma English). Hours per visit-kesennuma: 8:30–17:15; a Japanese notice says staff are absent Wed/Thu. Rental bikes here. **Verify phone and hours.**
2. **気仙沼市観光サービスセンター — Kesennuma City Tourism Service Center.** 2F of **気仙沼 海の市 (Umi no Ichi)**, next to the fish market. Rental bikes; hours vary by season.
3. **気仙沼大島ウェルカム・ターミナル — Oshima Welcome Terminal.** 浦の浜151-2外, 09:00–17:00, tel 0226-28-9253.
