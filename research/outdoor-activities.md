## 1. NAMED NATURAL SIGHTS & OUTDOOR SPOTS

Central Kesennuma reference points: **JR Kesennuma Station** (Ofunato Line / Kesennuma Line BRT), the **Uminoichi / Fish Market** waterfront, and **PIER 7** (cruise dock). Note the station is not on the waterfront — the port district is a separate cluster. Sanriku Expressway ICs are the key driving reference: **Urashima-Oshima IC (浦島大島IC)**, **Kesennuma-Shikaori IC (気仙沼鹿折IC)**, **Karakuwa-Hantō IC (唐桑半島IC)**, **Otani-Kaigan IC (大谷海岸IC)**, **Iwaisaki IC (岩井崎IC)**.

### 1.1 Oshima Island — 気仙沼大島
**EN:** Kesennuma Oshima / Oshima Island. Nicknamed "the Green Pearl" (緑の真珠).
**Why go:** Largest inhabited island in Tohoku **[SEARCH-ONLY — repeated on several sites, and "one of the largest" on the Japan Tourism Agency page; hedge the superlative]**. Since the bridge opened it's a drive-on / cycle-on island with a 235 m summit panorama, a Blue Flag beach, sea kayaking and camping — i.e. it can carry an entire day of an outdoor weekend by itself.
**Access from central Kesennuma:** see §3.

### 1.2 Mt Kameyama — 亀山
**EN:** Mt Kameyama.
**Elevation:** **235 m** **[CONFIRMED, three sources]**. Highest point on Oshima.
**Why go:** 360° panorama over Oshima, Kesennuma Bay, the ria coastline and the open Pacific; Mt Kinkasan visible on a clear day **[SEARCH-ONLY]**; night views of Kesennuma city, *isaribi* (squid-boat fishing lights) and stars **[SEARCH-ONLY — and note the facility closes 16:45, so night viewing is NOT a normal visitor option; do not imply it is]**.
**Access:** Monorail from the Kameyama parking-lot station, or on foot via the 遊歩道. Address 宮城県気仙沼市亀山3-2 **[CONFIRMED]**. Oshima Shrine (大島神社) sits on the mountain **[SEARCH-ONLY, via michinokutrail.com]**.

### 1.4 Kodanohama Beach — 小田の浜(海水浴場)
**EN:** Kodanohama Beach (also romanised **Odanohama** on the official English page — pick one and note the variant).
**Why go:** Half-moon arc of sand with clear, shallow, sheltered water on Oshima's east side; **Blue Flag** certified — one of very few in Japan; historically ranked **2nd nationally** in the Environment Ministry's "100 Best Bathing Beaches" special selection **[CONFIRMED on kesennuma-kanko.jp/kodanohama/ for the ranking; Blue Flag CONFIRMED via the 2026 beach page]**. Shallow and calm because an offshore island blocks the wind **[SEARCH-ONLY]**.
**2026 season [CONFIRMED]:** **18 July – 30 August 2026.** Hours **weekdays 9:00–16:00, weekends/holidays 9:00–17:00**. **Lifeguards on duty.** Showers **¥100/3 min**, changing rooms, toilets. **Parking ¥1,000/vehicle, 195 spaces.** ⚠VERIFY all.
*Note a conflict:* the spot page says **70** parking spaces; the 2026 season page says **195**. The 195 is probably seasonal overflow. **[UNVERIFIED]**
**Also:** SUP and surfing allowed; **jet skis and similar watercraft prohibited** **[CONFIRMED]** — useful "quiet beach" angle. Reopened for swimming in **2012** after the 2011 disaster **[CONFIRMED]**. Contact: Oshima branch **0226-28-3000**.
**Access:** **15 min by car from Urashima-Oshima IC**; in-season buses from Kesennuma Station, nearest stop **Asakane (浅根)**, ~5 min walk **[bus detail SEARCH-ONLY]**.

### 1.5 Cape Iwaisaki — 岩井崎
**[CONFIRMED — kesennuma-kanko.jp/iwaisaki/]**
**Why go:** Limestone cape on the mainland south coast with a natural **blowhole (潮吹き岩, shiofuki-iwa)** where waves force spray up through fissures — reportedly most dramatic at low tide **[low-tide detail SEARCH-ONLY; a good practical tip if you flag it as "reportedly"]**. Also a **Permian fossil site, ~250 million years old**, a reference locality with dozens of species — coral, bivalves, ammonites, crinoids — designated a **Miyagi Prefecture natural monument; collecting is prohibited** **[CONFIRMED]**.
**龍の松 (Ryū-no-Matsu, "the Dragon Pine"):** tsunami-damaged pine whose surviving twisted trunk resembles a rising dragon; the official site calls it a symbol of Iwaisaki's recovery **[CONFIRMED]**. Details **[SEARCH-ONLY, needs verification]**: ~2.5 m tall, ~1.7 m girth; it died, was cut into sections in July 2015, sent to Kyoto for preservation treatment, and re-installed February 2016. Registered as a **震災伝承施設 (disaster-memorial facility)** by MLIT Tohoku **[SEARCH-ONLY, thr.mlit.go.jp listing exists]**. Strong human-interest content, but verify before printing dimensions/dates.
**On site:** a **salt-making experience hall (塩づくり体験館)** **[CONFIRMED it exists; I did NOT confirm hours, price, or whether it takes walk-ins/English]**. There is also a statue of a sumo wrestler in the area **[UNVERIFIED — I asked and the page did not confirm it; do not publish]**.
**Access [CONFIRMED]:** by car **9 min from Otani-Kaigan IC** (from Sendai side) or **9 min from Iwaisaki IC** (from Iwate side). Nearest public transport: **Rikuzen-Kaijō (陸前階上) on the JR Kesennuma Line BRT**, then ~**40 min on foot / ~5 min by car** **[the 40-min walk figure is SEARCH-ONLY]**. Parking **30 spaces**. Address 宮城県気仙沼市波路上岩井崎1. Contact 0226-27-5410 (Kaijō/Hashikami branch).
*Older drive times from search, less reliable:* 20 min from Otani-Kaigan IC, 15 min from Iwaisaki IC, 1h20 from Ichinoseki IC. The official page's 9/9 min presumably reflects newer expressway sections. **Prefer the official figures.**

### 1.6 Mt Anba — 安波山
**EN:** Mt Anba / Anbayama.
**Elevation:** **239 m** **[CONFIRMED, kesennuma-kanko.jp/anbasan/ and japan-guide]**.
**Why go:** The in-town mountain directly behind Kesennuma City Hall — the closest thing to a "walk from the station" viewpoint, overlooking the bay, the city and out to Oshima. Name derives from praying for **safe voyages and big catches** **[SEARCH-ONLY]** — a nice line for a port-town itinerary.
**Viewpoints [SEARCH-ONLY, from yakei.jp / nightview.info / miyagi-map.com — the official page confirms only the *names*]:** three viewpoints at roughly **5, 10 and 15 min** above the 5th-station car park:
- **ひのでのてらす (Hinode-no-Terasu, "Sunrise Terrace")** — ~5 min, easiest; **the path is lit with footlights**, which is why this is the standard night-view option.
- **ほしのてらす (Hoshi-no-Terasu, "Star Terrace")** — ~10 min viewing deck; reportedly a slightly wider field of view than the summit itself.
- **Summit** — highest of the three, looks down on Kesennuma Port.
The two terrace names ARE confirmed on the official page **[CONFIRMED]**; the times, lighting and relative views are **[SEARCH-ONLY]**.
**Access [CONFIRMED]:** parking **20 vehicles**; nearest BRT **Kashiori-Karakuwa (鹿折唐桑)**; by car ~**10 min from Urashima-Oshima IC**. Located 宮城県気仙沼市町裏 (Machiura).
**japan-guide [CONFIRMED]** describes it as a 239 m mountain with an overlook car park and a **15-minute walk to the summit**. Consistent.
**Cycling [CONFIRMED, visit-kesennuma]:** ~**20 min by bike** from the station-area Tourist Information Center, vs **50 min on foot**. Useful — it means Mt Anba is genuinely car-free-accessible.

### 1.7 Karakuwa Peninsula — 唐桑半島
**EN:** Karakuwa Peninsula.
**Length:** ~**20 km** **[SEARCH-ONLY]**; the MCT club describes it as a **19 km peninsula walk** **[CONFIRMED]**.
**Why go:** The most dramatic stretch of ria coastline in the municipality, in **Sanriku Fukko (Reconstruction) National Park** — sea cliffs, marble outcrops, tsunami boulders, camellia tunnels, and the local vernacular architecture. The MCT club highlights the **"Karakuwa palaces" (唐桑御殿)** — grand houses built with deep-sea fishing money, "a unique aspect of Karakuwa with its many deep-sea fishermen" **[CONFIRMED]**. This is a distinctive, non-generic angle worth leading with.
**Access:** Karakuwa-Hantō IC on the Sanriku Expressway is the hub. Public transport is the weak point — see §4.5.

### 1.8 Ogama — 巨釜 (and Hanzo — 半造)
**[CONFIRMED — kesennuma-kanko.jp/ogama/]**
**Why go:** Signature viewpoint of the Karakuwa coast. The official text: *"リアス海岸独特の入り組んだ地形が連続し、波の浸食によって、奇岩怪岩が多く、荒々しい男性的な景観美"* — continuous intricate ria topography, wave-eroded into a mass of strange rock forms, a rugged, "masculine" scenic beauty. The name **Ogama = "giant cauldron"**, from water churning as if in a great pot; the offshore **Hachiman Rock (八幡岩)** looks like its lid **[CONFIRMED]**.
**巨釜 and 半造** are usually treated as a pair (**巨釜半造**) with a linking walking path (巨釜半造遊歩道). Hanzo has a **rest house (半造レストハウス)** which is the Miyagi Olle finish point **[SEARCH-ONLY]**.
**Access [CONFIRMED]:** car — **11 min from Karakuwa-Hantō IC** (Sendai side); **20 min from Rikuzentakata-Osabe IC** or **1h40 from Ichinoseki IC** (Iwate side). Bus — Osaki Line (御崎線) to the "巨釜半造入口 / Ogama-Hanzo entrance" stop then ~**20 min walk**; from JR Kesennuma Station, alight **Keshōzaka (化粧坂)**, ~10 min walk. **Parking 30 spaces.** ⚠The two bus descriptions are confusing; verify with a timetable.

### 1.9 Oreishi — 折石
**[CONFIRMED — kesennuma-kanko.jp/ogama/]**
**EN:** Oreishi ("the Broken Rock" / "Snapped Stone").
**What:** A **marble sea stack 16 m tall and 3 m wide**, standing vertically off the tip of Ogama.
**Why the name:** It was formerly called **天柱岩 (Tenchū-iwa, "Pillar of Heaven")**; the **1896 Meiji Sanriku tsunami snapped 2 m off its top**, and it was renamed 折石 ("broken stone"). **[CONFIRMED]** — note the official page says 明治29年 = 1896, and visit-kesennuma independently says 1896 **[CONFIRMED]**.
**Significance:** Described as a signature landscape of Sanriku Fukko National Park **[CONFIRMED]**.
This is your best single image + story pairing on the mainland: a rock that records a tsunami from 1896, on a trail that also passes boulders moved by the 2011 tsunami.
**Access:** Same as Ogama. On the Miyagi Olle course it is at the **8.0 km** mark **[CONFIRMED]**.

### 1.10 Dairiseki Coast — 大理石海岸
**EN:** Dairiseki Coast / "Marble Coast".
**[CONFIRMED — kesennuma-kanko.jp/大理石海岸/]**
**Why go:** On the **north** side of the Karakuwa peninsula near the Iwate border, facing **Hirota Bay (広田湾)** in a **calm, sheltered inlet** — the opposite character to Ogama's wild south-facing cliffs. Continuous marble reefs; white marble against deep blue water. Quality marble was **quarried here and reportedly used in the Mitsukoshi department store in Tokyo** **[CONFIRMED as claim on official page — it says "reportedly", so hedge it]**; no longer quarried.
**Access [CONFIRMED]:** car — **10 min from Karakuwa-Hantō IC**, or **10 min from Rikuzentakata-Osabe IC**, or **1h23 from Ichinoseki IC**. Bus — Osaki Line to **Obaragi (小原木)**, ~5 min walk; from JR Kesennuma Station nearest stop **Keshōzaka**, ~10 min walk. **Parking only 10 spaces** — worth flagging, it's a small site.
Contact 0226-32-3029 (Karakuwa branch).
*⚠ The fetched page's romanisation garbled "Karakuwa" as "Karikushi/Karakuwa" and the address as "Iwate-gun" — that's the summariser, not the source. Use 宮城県気仙沼市唐桑町 for the address and re-verify the exact district.*

### 1.11 Cape Osaki — 御崎
**EN:** Cape Osaki (Osaki-misaki). Read **おさき**.
**[Mostly SEARCH-ONLY — I did not successfully fetch the dedicated official page; kesennuma-kanko.jp/御崎（八隻曳）/ exists]**
**Why go:** The **tip of the Karakuwa peninsula**, where the open Pacific breaks hard against the rocks — described as 勇壮 (heroic/wild) scenery. Sub-features: **陽沼・陰沼 (Hinuma/Kagenuma)**, **八隻曳 (Hassekihiki)** and **児置島 (Koki-shima)** **[SEARCH-ONLY]**.
**八隻曳 (Hassekihiki):** a broad, gently sloping shelf of **black slate, reportedly 30 m wide × 50 m long**, also called **千畳敷 ("thousand-tatami expanse")**; legend says the deity of Osaki Shrine came ashore in **eight boats** (hence the name) **[SEARCH-ONLY]**.
**遊歩道:** From the Osaki car park, walk **~500 m south through red pine forest** to reach the cape **[SEARCH-ONLY]**.
**Osaki Shrine (御崎神社):** **over 1,000 years old**, set in **thick laurel forest** **[CONFIRMED — both miyagiolle.jp/en and visitmiyagi.com]**. Nearby: the Karakuwa Peninsula Visitor Center, a **whale mound (鯨塚, kujira-zuka)**, and shrines associated with **safe childbirth and child welfare** **[the latter CONFIRMED on visit-kesennuma; kujira-zuka SEARCH-ONLY]**.
**Position on Miyagi Olle [CONFIRMED]:** Osaki Shrine at **0.4 km**, Cape Osaki at **0.8 km** from the Visitor Center. So the cape is a very short, easy walk from a staffed facility with parking — this makes it the single most family-friendly "wild Pacific" viewpoint in Karakuwa.

### 1.13 Mt Tokusenjo — 徳仙丈山
**[CONFIRMED — kesennuma-kanko.jp/tokusenjo/]**
**EN:** Mt Tokusenjo.
**Elevation: 711 m** **[CONFIRMED]**. Straddles Kesennuma and former Motoyoshi; near the Iwate border; described as the second-highest peak in the area **[SEARCH-ONLY]**.
**Why go:** **Reportedly ~500,000 wild azaleas** (ヤマツツジ *Rhododendron kaempferi* and レンゲツツジ *R. molle*) blanketing the mountain — billed as **Japan's largest azalea stand**, with the contrast of red azaleas against the Pacific as the money shot. **[CONFIRMED for 50万本 as "reportedly" (とも言われます) — the source itself hedges, so hedge it too. "Japan's largest" is a local claim; attribute it.]**
**Season: mid-to-late May.** **[CONFIRMED]** The city runs a bloom-status page (開花情報) each year — https://kesennuma-kanko.jp/blooming/ — worth linking, it's live for 2026 **[SEARCH-ONLY]**.
**Walking times [CONFIRMED]:** trailhead → summit **~40 min at a leisurely pace**; **~15 min to the first viewpoint**; allow **1.5–2 hours round trip** including descent. Toilets at the trailhead.
**Elevations along the way [SEARCH-ONLY, from jalan/yamap]:** Kesennuma-side trailhead **521 m** (~7th station), first viewpoint **600 m** (~8th), top of the "azalea avenue" **638 m** (~9th), summit **711 m**. Described as beginner-friendly. Consistent with the official 40-min figure.
**Access [CONFIRMED]:** ~**30 min by car from central Kesennuma**. **Shuttle bus operates mainly at weekends during the bloom season, mid-May to early June.** ⚠VERIFY annually.
**Verdict for copy:** effectively **car-required and May-only**. Outside mid-May it's a mountain with no flowers and no shuttle. Do not put it in a generic year-round itinerary.

### 2.1 Hiking — Michinoku Coastal Trail (みちのく潮風トレイル / MCT)

**The trail overall [CONFIRMED via m-tc.org/en/]:** long-distance nature trail across **4 prefectures and 29 municipalities**, linking the Sendai plains, ria coastline, coastal terraces and more. Managed by **NPO Michinoku Trail Club**, with maps, GPS data, a Companion Book and access info.

**Area 5 — Kesennuma to Kitakami-machi, Ishinomaki [ALL CONFIRMED via m-tc.org/en/explore/hike/scenery/area-5/]:**
- **Kesennuma carries 102 km of the MCT** — second only to Ishinomaki, and **the longest continuous distance within a single municipality on the whole trail.** Excellent, quotable, and specific.
- **88 km of that is road walking.** ⚠Important honesty point for an outdoor itinerary — this is not 102 km of wilderness path.
- **Oshima Island: 18 km.**
- **Karakuwa Peninsula: a 19 km peninsula walk**, featuring the "Karakuwa palaces."
- **Natural trail option: Ogama → Karakuwa Visitor Center, around 7 km, clifftop, with ups and downs but well maintained.** ← **This is the single best short MCT day-hike recommendation in the report.** It is the scenic core, it's explicitly described as well maintained, and it's short enough for a weekend.
- Oshima is accessed via **Uranohama Kesennuma Oshima Welcome Terminal**; Mt Kameyama gives a panorama of the island and the **Karakuwa Strait** from **235 m**.

**Oshima loop [ALL CONFIRMED via michinokutrail.com/kesennuma-oshima]:**
- **~18.2 km**, **1 day (or overnighter)**, **difficulty: Intermediate**, **530 m ascent / 550 m descent**, **start and finish at Kesennuma Oshima Ohashi Bridge**.

Use it for route shape and feel; verify the Kameyama specifics.

**A 1-day MCT Karakuwa course [CONFIRMED via visit-kesennuma.com/plan-your-trip/trekking/]:** **19.6 km, 6 hours 32 minutes, 15 numbered stops**, starting from **Karakuwa Sōgōshishomae bus stop (唐桑総合支所前)**. ### 2.10 Birdwatching — WEAK; do not oversell
**I found no dedicated birdwatching site, hide, guided tour, or species list for Kesennuma.** Say so, or omit the topic. What I did find:
- **Umineko (ウミネコ, black-tailed gull, *Larus crassirostris*) is the official bird of Kesennuma City** — a resident (留鳥) species, traditionally seen as encouraging boats out of the harbour and welcoming them home. **[SEARCH-ONLY, kesennuma-kanko.jp/umineko-no-koto/]** Charming and locally specific.

## 3. OSHIMA ACCESS

**The bridge — 気仙沼大島大橋 (Kesennuma Oshima Ohashi)**, nicknamed **鶴亀大橋 (Tsurukame Ohashi)** **[bridge-stop name CONFIRMED; the nickname is implied by the "Tsurugaura/Tsurukame Ohashi" bus stops]**
**[CONFIRMED via kesennuma-kanko.jp/oshimaohashi/]**
- Opened **spring 2019** (Heisei 31). michinokutrail.com and multiple sources: **2019** **[CONFIRMED]**. Precise date: **7 April 2019** **[SEARCH-ONLY, from the Travel Watch article on the ferry's final day]**.
- **Arch span 297 m** — among the largest in eastern Japan and reportedly **4th longest in the nation** for its type. A large arch bridge, "rare nationwide."
- Construction: March 2017, a giant crane ship lifted the **2,700-tonne** central girder into place; featured on NHK's *Professional: Shigoto no Ryūgi*. Good colour.
- **Pedestrians and cyclists can cross.** There are **turnaround areas (転回場) at both ends, so you can park and walk across** — parking for **12 vehicles**. **[CONFIRMED]** This is a genuine free attraction and a good photo stop.
- By car: **3 min from Urashima-Oshima IC** or **5 min from Kesennuma-Shikaori IC**. By bus: 市民バス大島線, stops **Tsurugaura (鶴ヶ浦) / Tsurukame Ohashi**. **[CONFIRMED]**
- michinokutrail.com: *"the only permanent land access to the island"* — **[CONFIRMED]**, and true.

**The ferry ended in 2019 — CONFIRMED**
**大島汽船's scheduled Kesennuma–Oshima ferry route was abolished on 7 April 2019**, the same day the bridge opened, ending **about 100 years** of scheduled service; the final sailing ran that day. **Oshima Kisen continues as a sightseeing-cruise operator only.** **[CONFIRMED via ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大島汽船 + Travel Watch article, both via search — and independently corroborated by oshimakisen.com, which I fetched and which sells only sightseeing cruises, no ferry.]** Confidence: high. The "ありがとう大島汽船" farewell coverage is at travel.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1178718.html.
**Copy guidance:** there is **no scheduled passenger ferry to Oshima.** Some older guidebooks and blogs still describe one. The only boats now are sightseeing cruises, which do call at the Welcome Terminal wharf but on a cruise timetable, **not as transport** — do not let readers plan to "take the boat over."

**Bike** — best car-free option. **~10 km from the city centre to the bridge** **[CONFIRMED]**; e-assist bikes from three points with **one-way drop-off at the Welcome Terminal for +¥500** **[CONFIRMED]**; ~30 min TIC→bridge, ~10 min bridge→Terminal by bike **[CONFIRMED]**. See §2.5.

## 4. SEASONALITY AND DIFFICULTY

### 4.1 By season
**Spring (Mar–May)** — arguably the best hiking window. **Camellias in the Tsubaki Tunnel** **[CONFIRMED]**; **spring lilies and daisies** on the Olle **[CONFIRMED]**; **Mt Tokusenjo's ~500,000 azaleas peak mid-to-late May**, with a weekend shuttle mid-May to early June **[CONFIRMED]**. Sea kayaking/SUP season opens **March or April** depending on source **[CONFIRMED both, conflicting]**. Bay cruise season starts around **April** **[SEARCH-ONLY]**. No swimming.
**Summer (Jul–Aug)** — the only swimming window: **~18 July to 23/30 August** **[CONFIRMED]**. Everything is open; the new Kameyama Terrace is at peak novelty and **peak crowding** (opening-week crowd-control measures were in force **[CONFIRMED]**). Hottest and most humid; long coastal road-walking sections of the MCT will be unpleasant at midday. Festivals cluster here: **Minato Matsuri 1–2 August 2026** **[CONFIRMED it's listed]**.
**Autumn (Sep–Nov)** — strong for hiking and cruising: cruise runs **through November** **[CONFIRMED]**, kayak/SUP **through November** **[CONFIRMED]**. Culture peg: **sanma (Pacific saury) fishing** **[CONFIRMED, visitmiyagi]**. **Bonito Festival 7 October 2026** and **San-Rogaining 11 October 2026** **[CONFIRMED as listed events — verify details]**. Beaches closed to swimming.
**Winter (Dec–Feb)** — thinnest season. Bay cruise **not operating** (season ends November) **[CONFIRMED by inference from "through November"]**. Kayak/SUP closed **[CONFIRMED]**. But: **oysters are in season** and the *kaki-goya* oyster-hut lunch plan runs **Oct–May** **[CONFIRMED]**; **Osaki Campground is open year-round with winter camping by reservation** **[CONFIRMED]**; the Olle and coastal MCT are low-elevation and walkable. **[UNVERIFIED whether they're recommended in winter.]** Kameyama Terrace: "seasonal hour variations" noted **[CONFIRMED]** — verify winter hours and whether the monorail runs at all. Note the MCT has a **winter detour for Mt Tatsugane** further south **[CONFIRMED]** — evidence that winter conditions do close higher sections regionally.

### 4.4 Weather and safety caveats — all CONFIRMED unless noted
- **Monorail suspends in bad weather** — Kameyama can fail on the day. Walking up is the fallback (subject to §0.2).
- **Rental bikes are not lent out in the rain** **[SEARCH-ONLY]** — can kill a car-free Oshima plan.
- **Bay cruise runs only on selected weekends/holidays, not all of them** — check the operator.
- **Bear warning, entire MCT, issued 2026/06/24.**
- **Aftershock advisory, entire MCT, since the M7.7 quake of 20 April 2026.**
- **Tsunami:** the Olle course's own official page notes the area "on average has experienced a tsunami every 37 years." Coastal trails here have evacuation signage; visitors should know to move inland/uphill on any strong shaking or warning.
- **Beach sand loss is an active problem** — Oisehama has been unopenable for multiple years. Assume any beach's status can change.
- **Ria coastline = steep clifftop paths.** Ogama's own description is 荒々しい (rugged/wild). Sea-spray-wet rock at Iwaisaki, Ogama and Osaki. Blowhole and 千畳敷 rock shelves are places where people get too close to the water.
- **Facilities close early and irregularly.** Kameyama grounds close **16:45**; beach car parks **16:30**; Visitor Center **16:30, closed Tuesdays**; Oshima Kisen office **closed Tue–Wed**; rental bike points **closed Wednesdays**; Osaki campground office **closed Tuesdays**. **Tuesday and Wednesday are bad days to visit Kesennuma's outdoors.** That's a genuinely useful, non-obvious planning tip.
- Official English advice **[CONFIRMED]**: "Shops and restaurants on the island may have limited operating hours" — research ahead, wear comfortable shoes, bring layers, **carry cash** for independent vendors.
- **Toilets are limited at Kameyama Terrace** **[CONFIRMED]**.

### 4.5 The single biggest open question
**Public transport in Karakuwa and to Oshima at weekends.** Three official sources give three different pictures (Miyako Bus 45 min to Mikkamachi; Osaki Line to the Visitor Center; city-run demand-responsive transport for the Karakuwa area), and the official English page says the Oshima Citizen Bus is **weekdays only**. **A weekend outdoor itinerary depends entirely on resolving this.** Recommend the writer either (a) confirm current timetables with the Kesennuma Tourism Association (**0226-22-4560** area — ⚠I did not confirm this number; the numbers I did confirm are Oshima branch **0226-28-3000**, Karakuwa branch / Visitor Center **0226-32-3029**, city tourism division **0226-22-3438** **[SEARCH-ONLY for the last]**), or (b) write the itinerary around **e-bike + car/taxi** and note bus service as limited and requiring verification.

## 6. WRITER'S NOTES — what to be careful about

1. **Lead with Kameyama Terrace 360°.** It opened 19 July 2026, no English guidebook has it, and it converts the region's best viewpoint from a hike into a step-free monorail ride. This is your differentiator.
2. **Three things every existing English source gets wrong now:** the Kameyama lift/shuttle (replaced by the monorail), the Welcome Terminal shops (were closed, reopened 19 July 2026), and the Visitor Center's tsunami-simulation exhibit (closed).
3. **Don't state the MCT's total length as a single number** — official, semi-official and third-party sources say ~1,000 km, "over 900 km" and 1,025 km. "Roughly 1,000 km" is safe.
4. **Be honest that 88 of Kesennuma's 102 MCT km are road walking.** Steer readers to the **7 km Ogama → Visitor Center clifftop trail** or the **10 km Miyagi Olle** instead of implying the whole 102 km is scenic singletrack.
5. **Never publish a price from this report without re-checking.** Kayaking alone appeared at ¥6,000, ¥8,000 and ¥7,500 across three sources; the oyster tour is a ¥90,000–100,000 *per-boat charter* that would be catastrophic to present as a per-person price.
6. **Never publish beach dates without re-checking.** Kodanohama ran 19 Jul–15 Sep in 2025 and 18 Jul–30 Aug in 2026.
7. **Do not promise a scheduled ferry to Oshima.** It ended 7 April 2019. The only boats are sightseeing cruises on a partial weekend schedule.
8. **Resolve the weekend bus question before writing any car-free itinerary** (§4.5). If you can't, build the itinerary on e-bikes plus a taxi and say bus service is limited.
9. **Tuesdays and Wednesdays are bad days here** — the Visitor Center, cruise office, bike rental points and campground office all close on one or both.
10. **Include a brief, non-alarmist safety note**: an M7.7 quake struck offshore on 20 April 2026, the trail club has a live aftershock advisory and a June 2026 bear warning, and this coast averages a tsunami every 37 years by the Olle organisers' own reckoning. Tell readers to check https://m-tc.org/en/news/attention/ before hiking. This is the responsible framing, and it also happens to be good, credible travel writing.
11. **Under-supported topics — say so or drop them:** birdwatching (no site, hide, tour or species list found — only the *umineko* city-bird story and gull-feeding on boats), and the "singing sand" claim for Jūhachihari-hama.
